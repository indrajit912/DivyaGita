About DivyaGita

A modern open platform dedicated to studying and understanding the Bhagavad Gita.

The Platform

DivyaGita is an open platform dedicated to studying and understanding the Bhagavad Gita. Its purpose is to preserve the original Sanskrit verses and their transliterations while allowing scholars, students, and devotees from all over the world to contribute thoughtful explanations, commentaries, and interpretations.

Through this collaborative repository, we seek to make the profound, timeless insights of the Gita accessible to everyone in a clear, digital, and mobile-friendly format, encouraging deep study and personal reflection.

Philosophical Significance of the Bhagavad Gita

The Bhagavad Gita ("The Song of the Lord") is a 700-verse Hindu scripture that is part of the epic Mahabharata (chapters 23–40 of Bhishma Parva). It is structured as a dialogue between Prince Arjuna and his guide and charioteer, Lord Krishna.

At the start of the Dharma Yuddha (righteous war) between the Pandavas and the Kauravas, Arjuna is overwhelmed by moral dilemmas and grief about fighting his own kinsmen. He turns to Lord Krishna for counsel. Krishna's answers and discourse constitute the Bhagavad Gita.

Core Philosophical Teachings
  • Nishkama Karma (Selfless Action): The core philosophy of performing one's duty (dharma) without any attachment to the results or fruits of the action. As Krishna famously says: "You have a right to perform your prescribed duty, but you are not entitled to the fruits of action."
  • The Eternity of the Soul (Atman): Realizing that the physical body is temporary, while the soul is eternal, indestructible, and never dies.
  • Pathways to Liberation (Yoga): The Gita lays down three paths to attain union with the Supreme:
    • Karma Yoga (the path of action)
    • Jnana Yoga (the path of knowledge)
    • Bhakti Yoga (the path of devotion)

Lord Krishna reveals himself as the Supreme Cosmic Spirit (Vishvarupa) and teaches that through devotion, self-discipline, and dedicated action, any individual can transcend suffering and attain ultimate liberation (Moksha).
